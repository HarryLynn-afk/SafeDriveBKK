About SafeDrive BKK

SafeDrive BKK is a hackathon demo that turns Bangkok crash data into practical decision support: where road risk concentrates, when pressure rises, and how a safer route can differ from the fastest one.

2,669 Bangkok crashes analyzed

Official 2024 Bangkok crash records from the Thai Ministry of Transport.

83.8% Holdout crash capture

On a Jan-Sep versus Oct-Dec 2024 holdout, flagged high-risk grids captured 83.8% of later mapped crashes.

17.5x Lift vs random

The model surfaces risky zones far more effectively than random zone selection.

Oct-Dec Validation period

Performance is summarized on a held-out Oct-Dec 2024 period rather than only a random split.

What Judges Should Know

SafeDrive BKK is built around one simple idea: public safety data is most useful before a trip starts. Instead of only describing where crashes already happened, the demo highlights persistent danger zones that can inform routing, planning, and public communication.

The core signal is concentration. Just 126 high-risk zones capture 88.4% of mapped crashes, which is why the product focuses on targeted risk explanation, route-aware guidance, and hotspot storytelling rather than claiming every street can be predicted with equal precision.

What The Demo Includes Today

The current build already includes four connected surfaces: a citywide risk map, a safer-versus-faster Trip Planner, an analytics page for explaining the data story, and a Safety Assistant for grounded Q&A.

For the hackathon demo, the goal is not to claim a finished production system. The goal is to show a credible working prototype with a clear user need, measurable signal, and responsible framing around what the model can and cannot do.

How It Works

Risk Prediction Model

  • 44 engineered features per 1 km2 grid zone
  • Spatial, temporal, severity, and vehicle-mix inputs
  • Trained on 2,089 records and tested on 569
  • 17.5x lift over random zone selection

Thai NLP Pipeline

  • Thai crash-report text is tokenized with pythainlp
  • TF-IDF and Logistic Regression support cause classification
  • Six cause categories feed analytics and assistant answers
  • Used to interpret crash-report language, not replace source data

Demo Experience

  • Interactive citywide risk heatmap
  • Analytics views designed for live narration
  • Trip Planner comparing faster and safer options
  • Safety Assistant grounded in Bangkok crash data

Trust, Privacy & Limitations

Data Source

  • Thai Ministry of Transport, 2024
  • 23,952 nationwide records in the source release
  • 2,669 Bangkok crashes with 91.5% GPS coverage
  • Public official government data, not synthetic examples

Privacy

  • No personal identities are collected or stored
  • Trip locations are used in memory for route guidance
  • No user profiling, behavioral targeting, or driver scoring
  • Outputs are based on aggregate crash patterns from public data

Limitations

  • Predictions are based on 2024 historical crash patterns
  • The system indicates relative risk, not guaranteed safety
  • 1 km grid resolution still hides street-level variation
  • Not appropriate for legal, insurance, or enforcement decisions

Known Data Gaps

  • Not every crash is reported into the source system
  • 8.5% of records lack precise GPS coordinates
  • Coverage reflects Bangkok urban conditions more than rural ones
  • Road conditions and enforcement may have changed since 2024

Reasonable Uses

Route planning awareness for daily commuters and fleet operators.

Infrastructure planning and resource targeting around persistent hotspots.

Public communication that explains concentrated high-risk corridors.

Decision support that helps teams ask better safety questions.

Not What This Demo Should Be Used For

Insurance pricing, legal judgment, or enforcement decisions.

Stigmatizing neighborhoods or claiming a location is "safe" in absolute terms.

Replacing driver judgment, traffic law, or real-time road awareness.

Assigning liability after an accident.
