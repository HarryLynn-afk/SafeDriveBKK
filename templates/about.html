While the government tracks accidents after they happen, RoadRisk Bangkok uses historical data to identify persistent danger zones before drivers enter them. Our system analyzes 2024 accident patterns to provide advance awareness, enabling drivers to exercise extra caution in high-risk areas. The goal is not to prevent all accidents, but to reduce risk through informed decision-making.

🔬 Methodology

Data Sources

• Primary: Thai Ministry of Transport Open Data Portal - 23,952 road accidents nationwide in 2024

• Coverage: 2,669 accidents within Bangkok metropolitan area (13.5-14.0°N, 100.3-100.9°E)

• Quality: 91.5% GPS accuracy, 100% datetime parsing success

Risk Calculation Method

Spatial Aggregation: Bangkok divided into 2,418 grid cells (1km × 1km resolution)

Risk Score Formula: Risk = min(100, (Total Accidents × 1.2) + (Temporal Boost × 2.5))

Formula dynamically weights historical accident frequency with temporal danger patterns

Scores capped at 100 to maintain interpretable 0-100 scale

Classification Thresholds:

• High Risk (🔴): Score ≥55 (typically 5+ accidents per grid)

• Medium Risk (⚠️): Score 30-54 (typically 2-4 accidents per grid)

• Low Risk (🟢): Score <30 (typically 0-1 accidents per grid)

Temporal Analysis

• Peak danger day: Saturday (16.9% of accidents - 1.52× average)

• Peak danger hour: 10:00-10:59 AM (highest concentration)

• Secondary peak: 3:00-6:00 PM (rush hour - matches 2026 New Year data)

Technology Stack

Backend: Python 3.11, Flask, Pandas, NumPy, Folium

Machine Learning: XGBoost (gradient boosting), scikit-learn, 44-feature pipeline

Routing: OSRM (Open Source Routing Machine) for realistic road paths

Frontend: Bootstrap 5, Leaflet.js for interactive maps, JavaScript for real-time simulation

Geospatial: Nominatim for address autocomplete, Haversine distance calculations
