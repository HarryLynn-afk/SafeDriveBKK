SafeDrive BKK

A Bangkok road-safety decision support demo that turns official crash records into clear hotspot insights, safer route context, and judge-friendly evidence of where intervention can matter most.

What Judges Should Notice

SafeDrive BKK is built around one clear insight: Bangkok crash risk is concentrated, not evenly distributed. Instead of treating every road segment the same, the system highlights the smaller set of zones where crash pressure repeatedly shows up.

That concentration powers the product story. Just 126 high-risk zones contain 88.4% of mapped crashes, so the map, trip planner, analytics, and assistant can all point to the same practical message: focus attention where the data says risk is recurring.

How The Demo Works

Risk Prediction Layer

  • XGBoost model over 44 engineered features per roughly 1 sq km zone
  • Combines severity, spatial, temporal, and vehicle-mix signals
  • Trained on earlier 2024 data and checked against a later holdout window
  • Produces ranked risk tiers rather than claiming certainty about any single crash

Thai Language Layer

  • Thai tokenization on crash reports using PythaiNLP
  • TF-IDF with Logistic Regression classification
  • Six cause categories surfaced in the Safety Assistant and analytics views
  • Helps translate raw report language into usable safety themes

Demo Experience

  • Citywide hotspot map for spatial storytelling
  • Analytics dashboards for timing, area, and harm patterns
  • Trip Planner route comparison with safety context
  • Safety Assistant grounded in Bangkok crash data

Data, Trust & Limits

Data Source

  • Thai Ministry of Transport, 2024
  • 23,952 nationwide records in the source release
  • 2,669 Bangkok crash records, with most records mapped to coordinates for spatial analysis
  • Uses public official government data rather than scraped or private data

Privacy

  • No personal identity data is collected or stored in the demo
  • Route coordinates are used to compute guidance, not to build user profiles
  • No driver-level scoring, surveillance, or persistent tracking
  • Outputs are based on aggregate crash patterns from public records

Limitations

  • Predictions are based on 2024 historical data and may miss recent road changes
  • The system highlights relative risk, not guaranteed future outcomes
  • Zone-level outputs still hide street-by-street variation inside each area
  • Not appropriate for legal, insurance, or liability decisions

Known Coverage Gaps

  • Not every crash is reported into the source system
  • Some records lack precise GPS coordinates, which reduces mapping coverage
  • The current model is tuned to Bangkok urban conditions, not all of Thailand
  • Infrastructure, traffic enforcement, and construction conditions can shift over time

Appropriate Uses

[Yes] Route planning awareness for daily commuters and delivery drivers

[Yes] Fleet safety decision support and risk briefings

[Yes] Infrastructure planning and targeting of road-safety interventions

[Yes] Public communication around recurring high-risk corridors

Not Appropriate For

[No] Discriminatory insurance pricing based only on location

[No] Restricting access to neighborhoods or stigmatizing communities

[No] Replacing safe driving judgment, traffic law, or official navigation guidance

[No] Determining fault, liability, or legal responsibility after a crash
